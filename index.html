Course Objectives

  • Fundamental Goal: Applying AI/ML to 5G and Beyond. This course is designed to explore how machine learning-particularly deep learning-can be applied in modern wireless networks, especially in the context of 5G and beyond.
  • Core Focus: 5G/ORAN and Hybrid Routing Systems. We'll focus primarily on the 5G network architecture, especially on the Open Radio Access Network (ORAN). One of the central themes will be how to intelligently route models (i.e., model routing) in edge environments-selecting and placing ML models dynamically across the network. This intersects with both model routing and network routing, so students will learn how to design such hybrid systems from the ground up.
  • Key Topics and Emphasis on Edge-Side Challenges. The course will also touch on topics such as resource allocation, OFDM, and other essential concepts in edge wireless systems. While we'll briefly cover elements of the 5G core network (e.g., slicing strategies), the emphasis will be on edge-side challenges.

Prerequisites

(W)		 Paper Practicum 4 (Wi-Fi II): Inside 802.11 — Which Knobs Can We Still Redesign?
(Three student presentations, 15 min each + 5 min Q&A)
  1. Law: Towards Consistent Low Latency in 802.11 Home Networks (USENIX NSDI 2026) [Video][PDF]
    Why this paper: the queues, rate adaptation, and retries we met in the history lecture are exactly what cause latency spikes — so Law redesigns the link layer to bound them. It also exploits something only a video application can offer the link layer: a tolerance for losing a little.
    Presented by Ricardo Furst [Slides]
  2. BLADE: Adaptive Wi-Fi Contention Control for Next-Generation Real-Time Communication (USENIX NSDI 2026) [Video][PDF]
    Why this paper: we spent the history lecture on CSMA/CA — sense, wait, back off a random amount, transmit. That random amount comes from a contention window the standard essentially fixes. BLADE asks why, and adapts every transmitter's window to the contention it actually sees: over 5× lower tail latency under heavy contention and 90% fewer cloud-gaming stalls, on commercial APs.
    Presented by Mitchell Thai [Slides]
  3. SMUFF: Towards Line Rate Wi-Fi Direct Transport with Orchestrated On-device Buffer Management (USENIX NSDI 2024) [Video][PDF]
    Why this paper: two phones can already talk over Wi-Fi Direct at nearly a gigabit — so why does a file transfer never reach it? The bottleneck is not the PHY but the buffers stacked along the path inside the device: application, transport, socket, kernel, driver. Over one hop the sender can see every one of those buffers, so it can manage occupancy directly instead of guessing at congestion the way TCP must. Result: 94.7% of practical line rate.
    Presented by Gannon Wooley [Slides]

  1. CARTS: Cooperative and Adaptive Resource Triggering and Stitching for 5G ISAC (ACM SenSys 2026) [PDF][arXiv]
    Why this paper: real 5G NR ISAC, with no new waveform at all. The uplink already carries two reference signals the standard put there for demodulation and channel sounding — DMRS and SRS.   2. CommRad: Context-Aware Sensing-Driven Millimeter-Wave Networks (ACM SenSys 2024) [Video][PDF][arXiv]
    Why this paper: the easiest entry point of the three, and the one with a talk video. A mmWave beam is narrow, so a walking user or a passing body breaks the link. CommRad puts a radar next to the base station and lets the two help each other — the radio tells the radar where to look, the radar keeps the beam on the user: 2.5× median and 8× 20th-percentile throughput.
    Presented by Jimmy Moreno
  3. 2FiA: Towards WiFi Sensing-Based Authentication with Unique Biometrics (IEEE S&P 2026) [Artifact][Award][Slides]
    Why this paper: the far end of the idea. The CSI we met in the Wi-Fi lectures — the calibration data the chip already exposes — carries your breathing, and underneath it your heartbeat. 2FiA turns those two into an authentication factor, using the heartbeat precisely because it is the harder one to imitate.

21 11/4
(W)		 Paper Practicum 8 (Edge AI I): Making AI Fit on the Device
(Three student presentations, 15 min each + 5 min Q&A)
  1. ElastiLM: Elastic On-Device LLM Service (ACM MobiCom 2025) [Video][Slides][PDF][Code]
    Why this paper: the easiest entry point of the three. One LLM on the phone now serves every app, but a keyboard suggestion and a document summary have very different deadlines. ElastiLM shrinks the model and the prompt per request to meet each one: up to 14.83% higher accuracy than seven baselines (10.45% on average), for less than 1% switching overhead, on real smartphones.
    Presented by Ty Officer
  2. D2MoE: Dual Routing and Dynamic Scheduling for Efficient On-Device MoE-based LLM Serving (ACM MobiCom 2025) [Video][PDF]
    Why this paper: model routing, the theme of this course, inside a single model. A Mixture-of-Experts LLM runs only a few experts per token, but all of them still have to live somewhere. D2MoE's router picks both which expert to run and at what precision, and its scheduler loads experts from storage while the processor computes: up to 1.39× throughput and 53% lower peak memory than current on-device frameworks, at INT8-level accuracy. Tested on a laptop GPU and a Jetson Orin rather than a phone.
    Presented by Sasha Pernica
  3. KVSwap: Disk-aware KV Cache Offloading for Long-Context On-device Inference (ACM MobiSys 2026) [Video][PDF][Code]
    Why this paper: the memory wall of long conversations. Every token an LLM has read leaves an entry in its KV cache, and on a phone or an embedded board the CPU and GPU share one memory, so there is nowhere left to spill it. KVSwap keeps the full cache on flash storage, predicts which few entries the next steps will need, and fetches them while it computes. The official talk is short (about 7 minutes), so plan to work mostly from the paper.
    Presented by Javier Rodriguez
22 11/9
(M)		 Paper Practicum 9 (Edge AI II): Where Should Intelligence Run?
(Three student presentations, 15 min each + 5 min Q&A)
  1. SAIL: Redesigning Collaborative Language Inference with a Single Server-to-Mobile Handoff (ACM MobiSys 2026) [Video][PDF]
    Why this paper: the phone's small model is fast but often wrong, the server's large model is right but a network round trip away, and earlier hybrids pay that round trip again and again while they generate. SAIL pays it once: the server writes only the hard opening tokens, then hands off to the phone to finish. Up to 76% higher accuracy than the phone's model alone, keeping 98.9% of the server model's accuracy within the latency target. The official talk is under 5 minutes, so work mostly from the paper.
    Presented by Kelsey Li
  2. SMEC: Enabling SLO-Aware 5G Multi-Access Edge Computing with SMEC (USENIX NSDI 2026) [Video][Slides][PDF][Code]
    Why this paper: "move it to the edge" is not a latency guarantee. Measuring commercial 5G edge deployments, the authors find deadlines missed constantly, because both the RAN scheduler we met in the XG lectures and the edge server are shared and blind to deadlines. SMEC puts a deadline-aware manager at each, reading what it needs from standard 5G protocols, and the two never have to coordinate: 90–96% of deadlines met versus under 6% for existing approaches, and up to 122× lower tail latency.
    Presented by Colin Largen
  3. Confidant: Customizing Transformer-based LLMs via Collaborative Training on Mobile Devices (ACM MobiCom 2025) [Video][PDF]
    Why this paper: from inference to training. Fine-tuning an LLM is too big for one phone, so Confidant splits one model into slices across the phones, tablets and laptops in a home and trains them as a pipeline, balancing each device's CPU and GPU and surviving devices that drop out: Phi-2 (2.7B) fine-tuned in 40.1 hours on three consumer phones. Mind the difference from federated learning, where every device trains a whole copy on its own data; here the devices share one model.
    Presented by Maxime Hildebrand

(W)		 Paper Practicum 10 (Edge Agents): From Perception to Action
(Three student presentations, 15 min each + 5 min Q&A)
  1. Modality Plug-and-Play: Runtime Modality Adaptation in LLM-Driven Autonomous Mobile Systems (ACM MobiCom 2025) [Video][PDF][Code]
    Why this paper: perception comes first. A car or robot carries many sensors, but feeding every one into an LLM is too expensive, and the useful ones change with the situation — the camera by day, LiDAR at night. Modality Plug-and-Play connects each sensor to the LLM's last blocks so modalities can be swapped in and out at runtime: 3.7× fewer FLOPs and 30% less memory than the best baselines, adapting in minutes from a few hundred samples.
    Presented by Vikram Rao
  2. TimelyLLM: Time-sensitive LLM Serving System for Physical-I/O Limited Agents (ACM MobiSys 2026) [Video][PDF][Code][Award]
    Why this paper: a robot acts out a command far more slowly than the LLM writes it, so waiting for the whole answer before moving wastes time, and a server shared by many robots wastes it for all of them. TimelyLLM releases executable commands early and schedules the rest of the generation into the time the robot spends moving: up to 1.52× better time utility and 84% less waiting, on workloads from real drones, robot arms and quadrupeds. Best Paper Runner-Up at MobiSys 2026.
    Presented by Sanai Andrews
  3. VeriSafe Agent: Safeguarding Mobile GUI Agent via Logic-based Action Verification (ACM MobiCom 2025) [Video][PDF][Code]
    Why this paper: the last step of the loop, where a mistake becomes real. An agent that taps the wrong button or sends the wrong message on your phone cannot take it back. VeriSafe Agent turns your instruction into a formal logic specification and checks every proposed action against it before the action runs: 94.33–98.33% verification accuracy and 90–130% more tasks completed. An accurate model and a reliable system are not the same thing.
    Presented by Pedro De Lana

(M)		 Paper Practicum 11 (Edge Privacy): What the Signal, the Features, and the Cache Give Away
(Three student presentations, 15 min each + 5 min Q&A)
  1. Poison to Cure: Privacy-preserving Wi-Fi Multi-User Sensing via Data Poisoning (ACM MobiCom 2025) [Video][PDF]
    Why this paper: the flip side of our Wi-Fi sensing lectures. If CSI can reveal your breathing and who you are, it can also reveal what you type. Poison to Cure lets a user protect themselves with no hardware change: the calibration data they hand to the sensing model is deliberately poisoned, so the model fails on private activities (accuracy down by more than 76%) while ordinary ones still work.
    Presented by Noah Seay
  2. FaceObfuscator: Defending Deep Learning-based Privacy Attacks with Gradient Descent-resistant Features in Face Recognition (USENIX Security 2024) [Video][PDF]
    Why this paper: splitting inference between device and server does not make it private. Sending features instead of images sounds safe, but the authors first show deep-learning attacks rebuilding faces from the "protected" features of existing schemes. Their defense strips the visual detail recognition does not need and mixes in features those attacks cannot invert: about 90% better privacy protection for a 0.3% accuracy cost, deployed at a university for more than 100,000 people.
    Presented by Ethan Anderson
  3. KV-Cloak: Shadow in the Cache: Unveiling and Mitigating Privacy Risks of KV-cache in LLM Inference (NDSS 2026) [Video][PDF][Code]
    Why this paper: the KV cache from Practicum 8 again, this time as a leak. The cache that makes LLM serving fast holds enough of your prompt that an attacker who reads it can reconstruct what you typed, and the paper shows three ways to do it. KV-Cloak scrambles the cache with a reversible obfuscation, reducing those reconstructions to noise at a latency cost mostly under 1%.
    Presented by Tyler Fink

Grading Policy

  • Assignments: 45%
    There will be several homework assignments (written and coding-based) spaced out over the course of the semester. All assignments are equally weighted. Submission instructions will be posted on GitHub Classroom and/or Canvas; most project submissions use GitHub.
  • Paper Practicum (Reading & Presentation): 15%
    Each student will complete one in-class paper practicum on top-tier conference papers related to Future Edge Networks.
    1. Format: Individual presentation only (one person per practicum). The same format applies to both undergraduate and graduate students: 15 minutes presentation + 5 minutes Q&A.
    2. Scheduling: Three papers are presented per class meeting. The practicum block runs for roughly seven weeks after the introductory lectures conclude.
    3. Paper selection: Papers will be assigned by the instructor. Students will first submit their preferred presentation dates (and optionally topics of interest). The instructor will then assign a paper to each student based on the course schedule and topic sequence.
    Important policy for paper practicum:
    1. In-person presentation only. No make-up or deferred practicums will be approved.
    2. Presentation time slots will be assigned in random order. Plan ahead when submitting your availability.
  • Final Project Report & Presentation: 40%
    1. Final presentation (15%) will take place at the end of the semester (dates subject to change). Each person will have 15 minutes for presentation and 5 minutes for Q&A.
    2. Demo (25%) You may choose whether or not to share the demo code. However, you must present a working demo during your project presentation.
    Important policy for final presentation:
    1. In-person presentation only. No make-up or deferred presentations will be approved.
    2. Multiple time slots will be assigned for each class in random order among groups. Plan ahead when submitting your availability.
  • Extra Bonus.
    Students are encouraged to prepare submissions to arXiv or major AI/ML/DM conferences based on their projects. Please make an appointment with the instructor prior to any submission plan for a comprehensive evaluation of the research topic. Each submission under the instructor's recognition will gain 2 points on the final grade.
